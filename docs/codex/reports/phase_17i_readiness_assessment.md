## 5. Identity and authorization assessment

### Supported modes and external access

`disabled`, `static_bearer` and `oidc_jwt` are the supported modes. Disabled/shared bearer operation does not identify individual users. Generic OIDC verifies operator-configured issuer/audience/purpose/roles using bounded RS256 JWKS verification; canonical tenant/owner derive from issuer/tenant/subject. Valid identities are registered by middleware on verification, **not admitted through a general invitation allowlist**. Issuer-side assignments plus an approved participant enrollment rule are needed for B; the financial evaluation allowlist is not an access allowlist.

The [Entra v2 profile](../../security/entra_v2.md) accepts a single configured tenant, delegated `access_as_user`, and only `PolyMind.L1Operator`; it maps to `PUBLICATION_MANAGE`. It does not grant tenant administration, but it still grants inappropriate publication authority for ordinary participants. Do not distribute the operator role, device-code session or token to participants. Device-code success is evidence about the operator's authentication only. A generic OIDC least-privilege route may be reusable for B, but its real participant identity, issuer enrollment and client lifecycle require validation. Cross-tenant federation is not established by the single-tenant Entra profile.

Principal disabling exists through trusted tenant-admin authority and rejects re-registration. Expiration/nbf and response snapshots are checked. Refresh-token acquisition, secure browser sessions, participant logout/global session invalidation, invitation enrollment and a participant administration interface are absent. JWKS key removal can remain effective only after cache expiry. No token, private key, credential or real identity was used in this assessment.

For A, authenticate the operator using the supported protected boundary, verify the consenting human in person, and record a pseudonymous session-to-operator attribution privately. A participant acts through the operator; the service must not pretend to hold an authenticated participant principal. Do not let the participant access terminal, raw API, host files or other sessions. An approved operator-facing presenter/runbook can reuse authorized services without adding participant credentials. This is an explicit different access model, not a waiver or OIDC bypass. Legacy `/query` and `/query/stream` remain denied in OIDC mode.

## 6. Consent, privacy and document eligibility

Participation consent, document-processing rights, personal-data lawful basis, provider transmission permission and retention acceptance are distinct decisions. Possession of a PDF is insufficient. Publicly downloadable is not equivalent to public domain or licensed for this processing; publications can contain personal information, restricted figures or contractual restrictions.

| Document class | First cohort decision | Required evidence / later boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Synthetic technical manuals and short reports | Preferred | Known authorship, no real personal data/secrets, frozen source/checksum and author permission. |
| Public-domain / openly licensed native technical material | Conditional preferred | Record source, license and permitted processing; screen for personal/confidential content and preserve attribution. |
| Public scientific publications | Conditional, not automatic | License/provider-processing permission, no sensitive case data, supported prose extraction. Figures/formula-dependent conclusions excluded. |
| Public regulatory/policy text | Conditional | Rights and nonpersonal-content check; research/usability only, no legal advice or compliance decisions. |
| User-owned nonconfidential technical documents | Prohibited in first cohort | A later controlled expansion needs explicit rights and provider consent, classification review, complete inventory and demonstrated erasure/retention controls. No private author document is accepted merely because it appears harmless. |
| Private/proprietary/confidential materials | Prohibited | Contractual authorization, privacy/security, provider terms and physical erasure unresolved. |
| Personal/sensitive records, health/financial/personnel data, children's documents | Prohibited | Separate specialist review and safeguards outside first cohort. No participant free-text upload channel. |
| Scanned/image-only, mixed essential visuals, complex tables/columns, encrypted/malformed/unsupported types | Excluded | OCR/layout not implemented; parser/quality and complete coverage not established. Refusal is a valid outcome, not support. |

Actual user-supplied documents should remain prohibited until physical erasure and the complete governance contract are demonstrated. This is a conservative pilot policy, not a claim that every nonpersonal public file legally requires erasure. An operator-selected public/synthetic document can be processed with disclosed retained artifacts if rights, retention and all other gates are accepted. It must not be misreported as validation of private-document service readiness.

### Required participant disclosure and withdrawal

Before any session, supply a short versioned notice describing the verification purpose and voluntary tasks; permitted documents and rights; operator and reviewer access; whether selected source text, prompts, generated child summaries and Q&A reach an external provider; selected region and any unresolved cross-jurisdiction transmission; retained source/extraction/checkpoints/digests/vectors/questions/feedback/audit metadata; exact retention periods or explicitly accepted retained-public-artifact policy; financial limits and who pays; extraction omissions, model errors, citation validity versus support; withdrawal/contact procedure; immediate access revocation versus incomplete erasure; and inability to recall delivered outputs or certify provider/backup deletion. Do not record real names/consent in Git or source-content analytics. No notice or consent is collected now.

### Portugal/EU review boundary

For personal data, review purpose/minimization and retention, lawful basis, transparency/rights, processor contracts, security, DPIA applicability and international transfers under GDPR Articles 5–6, 12–17, 28, 32, 35 and 44 onward. Participation consent is not automatically the lawful basis for every processing activity, and does not authorize third-party data. This is a checklist for qualified review, not a compliance conclusion. [GDPR primary text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng/).

Portugal's CNPD identifies the controller as the route for exercising rights and points to transparent notices; it also references national Law 58/2019. Provide an identified controller/contact and rights workflow before collecting participant identifiers or feedback. [CNPD rights guidance](https://www.cnpd.pt/cidadaos/direitos/). Obtain legal/privacy review of the notice, rights/processing permissions, provider terms and retention; involve the DPO where applicable. A DPO appointment is conditional on the activity, not implied by running a pilot. [CNPD DPO guidance](https://www.cnpd.pt/organizacoes/outras-obrigacoes/encarregado-de-protecao-de-dados/). Official sources were consulted on 12 November 2026; EUR-Lex direct full-text fetch encountered a JavaScript challenge, so article checks also relied on its indexed official text. Provider-specific residency, training use, abuse-monitoring retention, subprocessors and contractual erasure were **not verified**; they require the exact proposed service/terms before approval. Software tests cannot establish legal compliance.

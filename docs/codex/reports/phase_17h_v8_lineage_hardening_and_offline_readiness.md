## Exact observable violation and supported diagnosis

Use symbolic names to preserve privacy: `L` is the accepted chunk stage, `R` the
accepted immediate reducer, and `T` the current root. `L_i`, `R_i` and `T_i` are
the distinct positional claim IDs generated by `stable_id(str(stage), str(i))`.
Indices in this table are zero-based and match the actual saved output order.

The reconstructed root CONTROL has `stage_id=T`,
`claim_ids_in_order=[T_0, ..., T_7]`,
`required_child_links=[(R, R_0), ..., (R, R_7)]`, and four distinct original
`allowed_evidence_ids=[E_0, ..., E_3]` (symbolic names; actual ordering/UUIDs are
retained privately). Those source IDs originate in the accepted chunk evidence,
while the authorized direct claim IDs belong exclusively to its immediate reducer.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| `T_6` | `(R, R_6)` | `(R, L_6)` | No |
| `T_7` | `(R, R_7)` | `(R, L_7)` | No |

This table compares set membership; the application does not require a one-to-one
ordinal correspondence between new claims and child claims. Different valid
claim aggregation remains permitted, with complete coverage across the response.

CONTROL authorized exactly the eight `(R, R_i)` pairs, one immediate child stage
and its eight claims. All eight root claims used current `T` origins and correct
`T_i` positional IDs. All eight returned lineage stage IDs were `R`; every
returned lineage claim ID was instead the corresponding `L_i`. None was an
authorized `R_i`. Direct source-reference elements remained within
`CONTROL.allowed_evidence_ids`; correct original evidence cannot authorize an
invalid child pair. This is cross-level combination of identifiers, not a
current-stage identity, token, byte-budget, transport or source-ID failure.

The renderer and validator are consistent: CONTROL enumerates
`(child.result.stage_id, child_claim.claim_id)`, and `validate_result` constructs
its permitted link map from exactly those same immediate-child objects. The
validator checks each pair, prohibits duplicate pairs within a claim and then
requires complete child coverage across claims. It never validates independent
membership of stage IDs and claim IDs. A valid stage UUID paired with a different
level's otherwise valid claim UUID must fail. No application defect is demonstrated
by the reconstructed path; the observed cause is noncompliant model output.

V7 already required EVERY required child link and distinguished original source
evidence from generated material. Its current-stage identity instructions were
also correct and successfully followed. However, it did not explicitly say to
copy stage/claim pairs together, prohibit cross-level mixing, or explain that
earlier lineage remains reachable through the immediate-child pair. The supplied
child result contains its own earlier `(L, L_i)` links, so both levels' claim IDs
are visible. Confusion between those nested inherited links and direct output
links is a supported ambiguity hypothesis, not an observation of internal model
reasoning or proof of why this particular response was produced.

The same exact-pair rule applies to intermediate, structural and root reductions.
Any later intermediate/structural node can have grandchildren; multi-child nodes
also permit a distinct error of pairing one child's stage with another child's
claim. Chunk requests have no authorized child pairs at all. These exposures
are tested without introducing additional production stages or calls.
